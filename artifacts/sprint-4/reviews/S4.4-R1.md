## 3. Why Low Recall Is Accepted Here (Independent Judgment)

1. **What 1/6 measures.** Retrieval recall over 6 positive labeled examples with 205 expanded categories: whether the expected category name survived the metadata shortlist. It does not measure model classification accuracy — the smoke's single model decision (id 9001, confidence 0.4096) is one observed choice on the marker-injection example, reported without any correctness claim.
2. **Why the misses are honest.** The five misses (`meaningful-food→Food`, `meaningful-rent→Housing`, `meaningful-transport→Transport`, `meaningful-health→Health`, `boundary-long-state→Food`) remain recorded as expected-category retrieval misses. Nothing relabels them as "correct unknowns" from neural output. The sole hit is the example whose category name literally occurs in the description — exactly what name-only lexical retrieval should catch.
3. **Why it is not a contract FAIL.** The contract demands deterministic evidence-ranked selection with uncertainty and safe unknown on no-evidence — not a recall target. The failure mode is safe: `NO_METADATA_EVIDENCE` / `NO_FIT_CANDIDATES` return typed manual unknown with full omitted counts (`:219-253`), never a confident wrong id. No confident-misleading-candidate outcome was observed.
4. **Adoption risk carried forward, not hidden.** Name-only schema metadata cannot do semantic retrieval; S4.9 must weigh this against quality/latency/memory evidence on the phone. If S4.9 shows the shortlist systematically drops the right category, that is an adoption input — possibly schema enrichment (aliases/descriptions, S4.7-adjacent) or a different retrieval signal — not a reason to gate S4.4 on a target its contract never set.
5. **Not required here:** S4.5 background lifecycle, S4.6 suggestion UI, S4.7 remember/remove persistence, S4.9 phone metrics. The worker correctly leaves those out while keeping their seams (caller-owned rules, `resolveCurrent`, threading notes).

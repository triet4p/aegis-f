## 3. Detailed Inspection of Critical Areas

### 3.1 Ground-Truth Independence and Honest Error Preservation
The evaluation fixture set is defined in `tools/laya/expense_examples.json`. Review verified that the worker did not tune labels to match model predictions or suppress discrepancies:
- `boundary-long-state` ("Coffee with colleagues after standup plus dessert and extra pastries to take home for the whole team celebration event this Friday afternoon downtown near the lake side cafe with live music") was expected to classify as `Food`. On the phone, the model selected `Fun` (prob 0.7784, confidence 0.6976). The worker faithfully recorded this as `correct: false`, yielding a top-choice accuracy of 7/8 (87.5%).
- Out-of-domain expenses (`ambiguous-alex`, `unknown-gift`) both correctly resulted in `unknown` / `MODEL_NO_FIT` decisions.
- Confidently incorrect count was 0 at the threshold $\ge 0.80$, because the sole erroneous prediction had confidence 0.6976.

### 3.2 Candidate Retrieval Recall Bottleneck (1/6)
Evaluating 206 active synthetic categories against the 192-token head budget demonstrated a critical real-world bottleneck:
- In production, `CategoryEntity` metadata only stores `name`, with no aliases or expanded descriptions.
- The deterministic token-overlap candidate planner (`LayaCandidatePlanner`) was able to retrieve only 1 out of 6 expected positive categories (`boundary-mask-injection`, where the word "Food" explicitly occurred in the expense description).
- For the other 5 categories ("Food", "Housing", "Transport", "Health"), the expense descriptions ("Lunch at a cafe", "Monthly rent", "Grab ride to the airport", "Pharmacy antibiotics...", "Coffee with colleagues...") did not share literal words with category names, causing them to be excluded from the 5-option shortlist (`NO_METADATA_EVIDENCE`).
- The worker clearly concluded that broader model adoption is unsupported due to candidate retrieval recall, requiring future work to include synonyms/aliases before any automatic assignment could be considered.

### 3.3 Hardware Performance Metrics
Measurements taken on the physical HONOR ALT-LX2 (ARM64, Android 14, 4 KB page size):
1. **Cold Start:** $p50 = 10,535\text{ ms}$, $p95 = 11,159\text{ ms}$ ($n=3$). This accounts for releasing the session, recreating the ONNX Runtime session, running the contract probe, and running inference.
2. **Warm Inference:** $p50 = 903\text{ ms}$, $p95 = 914\text{ ms}$ ($n=20$). Once loaded, inferences consistently finish in under 1 second.
3. **Memory Footprint:** Peak PSS reached 1,620,186 KiB (~1.55 GiB). Baseline process memory was 1,610,339 KiB and final was 1,584,196 KiB. The sampling cadence achieved $p50 = 113\text{ ms}$ and $p95 = 220\text{ ms}$ over 79 samples.
4. **Lifecycle Responsiveness:** 360 scheduled main-looper heartbeat samples (20 ms target) showed a lateness of $p50 = 0\text{ ms}$, $p95 = 1\text{ ms}$, and $\max = 1\text{ ms}$. Concurrently launching `MainActivity` during background evaluation took 461 ms, confirming that inference running on `Dispatchers.Default` via `LayaInferenceLifecycle` does not starve the UI thread.
